Label the transport tissues
Figure 8.7: Transverse section of a root (labels removed).

Questions

Exam tip: root – xylem and phloem in the centre (xylem star-shaped); stem – vascular bundles in a ring near the outside edge, xylem on the inside of each bundle; leaf – in the veins. Xylem: water + mineral ions, support. Phloem: sucrose and amino acids.

    Questions

    Exam tip: root hairs give a large surface area → more uptake. Water enters by osmosis; mineral ions by active transport. Pathway: root hair cells → root cortex cells → xylem → mesophyll cells.
    EXPERIMENTAL SKILLS 8.1
    Investigating the pathway of water through a celery stalk

    You will need:

    • a fresh celery stalk with leaves
    • a beaker of water containing a coloured dye (eosin)
    • a sharp knife and a white tile; a hand lens
    Safety: Take care with the sharp knife – cut on a tile, away from your fingers. The dye stains skin and clothes.

    Method – click the steps in the correct order

      Extension: does temperature affect how fast the dye moves up?

      Choose a water-bath temperature and leave a fresh stalk in dye for 30 minutes. Then cut slices every 0.5 cm until no dye is seen. Do all four temperatures.

      °C
      Temperature / °CDistance moved by dye in 30 min / cm

      Questions

      Exam tip: the dye shows the xylem, because the dye travels with the water. In the stalk you see red dots in a ring near the outer edge (the vascular bundles); in the leaf the veins turn red. Warmer → more transpiration → dye moves up faster.
      ACTIVITY · EXAM-STYLE QUESTION 4
      Which leaf loses water fastest?

      Four leaves of the same size were hung up. Petroleum jelly (waterproof) was spread on some surfaces. Their mass was measured after one hour.

      0 min

      Questions

      Exam tip: most stomata are in the lower epidermis. Covering the lower surface stops most water vapour diffusing out. Show your calculation: % loss = (loss ÷ starting mass) × 100.
      EXPERIMENTAL SKILLS 8.2
      Using a potometer to compare rates of transpiration in different conditions

      Method (summary)

      • Cut the shoot under water, with a slanting cut, so no air enters the xylem.
      • Fit the shoot into the potometer; make it airtight with petroleum jelly.
      • Record the position of the meniscus every 2 minutes for 10 minutes.
      • Change one condition and repeat.

      Questions

      Exam tip: rate = (final reading − first reading) ÷ time, with units (mm per minute). Explain with kinetic energy (temperature), removal of water vapour / diffusion gradient (wind), shallow gradient (humidity). A potometer really measures water uptake.
      SUPPLEMENT · 8.4 TRANSLOCATION
      Sources and sinks
      Season:

      Click each labelled part to set it as source, sink or neither. Then check. Do both seasons.

      Removing a ring of bark (ringing)

      A ring of bark, including the phloem, is removed from a tree trunk. The xylem is deeper and is not damaged.

      Questions

      Exam tip: translocation = movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem from sources (release them) to sinks (use or store them). A store (root, tuber) is a sink while it builds up starch and a source when the starch is changed back to sucrose.
