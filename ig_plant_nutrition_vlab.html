EXPERIMENTAL SKILLS 6.1
Testing a leaf for starch

Leaves turn some of the glucose that they make in photosynthesis into starch. If we find starch in a leaf, that tells us it has been photosynthesising.

You will need:

  • a 250 cm³ beaker; a burner, tripod and gauze
  • a boiling tube; forceps; a glass rod
  • iodine solution, with a dropper
  • alcohol – collect this only when you are ready to use it
  • a fresh green leaf that has been photosynthesising; a white tile
Safety: Alcohol is flammable. Do not collect the alcohol until you have turned out your burner.

Method – click the steps in the correct order

    Questions

    Exam tip (method questions): boiling breaks down cell membranes so iodine can reach the starch; alcohol removes chlorophyll so the colour change can be seen; iodine turns blue-black with starch and stays orange-brown without it. Turn off the flame before using alcohol – it is flammable.

          EXPERIMENTAL SKILLS 6.7
          Investigating the effect of light and darkness on gas exchange in an aquatic plant

          Hydrogencarbonate indicator colours

          • purple – no carbon dioxide
          • red – a little carbon dioxide (as in ordinary air)
          • yellow – a lot of carbon dioxide

          Predict first (1 mark each): what colour will each tube be after 2 hours in the light?

          0 h

          Questions

          Exam tip: in the light, photosynthesis is faster than respiration, so the plant takes in more CO₂ than it gives out → CO₂ concentration falls → purple. In the dark there is only respiration → CO₂ rises → yellow. Tubes without a plant are the controls.
          SUPPLEMENT · LIMITING FACTORS
          Which factor is limiting the rate of photosynthesis?

          A limiting factor is a factor that is in short supply, and so limits how quickly the plant can photosynthesise. Switch on the curves and compare them.

          Figures 6.23–6.25: The effect of light intensity, carbon dioxide and temperature on the rate of photosynthesis (interactive).
          Exam tip: a factor is limiting if increasing it increases the rate. On the rising part of a curve, light intensity is limiting. On the flat part, light is not limiting – something else is (CO₂ concentration or temperature). Use the graph: “the rate increases from … to … when CO₂ increases, so CO₂ was limiting”.
