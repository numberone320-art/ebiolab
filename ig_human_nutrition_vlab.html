Exam tip: name the products: starch → maltose (then maltase → glucose); protein → amino acids; fat → fatty acids and glycerol. Pepsin works at pH 2; it stops in the duodenum because the pH is higher – write “active site changes shape / denatured”, not “the enzyme dies”.

Exam tip (workbook Q25, 6 marks): A stays blue – no maltose; B turns brick red – maltose present; amylase breaks down starch to maltose; maltose is a small, soluble molecule; it passes through the tubing by diffusion, from high to low concentration. Starch molecules are too large.

Part B · Capillary or lacteal?

🩸 Blood capillary

💧 Lacteal

Exam tip: villi and microvilli give a large surface area; the epithelium is one cell thick (short distance); many capillaries keep a concentration gradient. Glucose and amino acids → capillaries → hepatic portal vein → liver. Fatty acids and glycerol → lacteal. Most water is absorbed in the small intestine, some in the colon.
